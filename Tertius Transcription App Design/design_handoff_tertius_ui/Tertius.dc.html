A tau standing on two ruling lines — the guide-lines a scribe scored into the page before a single word was written.
On a 100-unit square. Crossbar 9u tall at full width; the second rule 9u tall, inset 12u each side, dropped to 29u — shorter and lighter, so it reads as ruling rather than a second stroke. Stem 16u, centred, running to the baseline. Below 24px the second rule is dropped entirely and the mark becomes a plain tau.

Recommended pairing
Cormorant SC · IBM Plex Sans · IBM Plex Mono
Cormorant only ever appears as display: the wordmark, status headlines, section labels. Every control, row, and number stays Plex. The high contrast makes the serif feel carved without any of it leaking into the interface.
Runner-up
Cardo
If you ever want real Greek in the interface — a verse, a name, a numeral — Cardo has the full polytonic set and Cormorant does not.
3b Eight marks, same direction Still a tau, still carved. Each moves one variable. Tell me a number.
Ⅰ · As built
The one in 2d, for comparison.
Ⅱ · Fully serifed
Flared terminals added rather than notched, plus a foot. Most letter-like; least mark-like.
Ⅲ · Tapered stem
The reed's pressure, narrowing to the page. Warmth without the pen literalism.
Ⅳ · Ruled
A second, shorter rule below the crossbar — the ruling lines of a page. Reads as writing, not just a letter.
Ⅴ · Counter-cut
The tau as void inside a solid block — relief carving. Strongest at 32px; heaviest overall.
Ⅵ · Spoken crossbar
The crossbar is a waveform. It can animate while a job runs and settle flat when done.
Ⅶ · Broken stem
A gap and a detached foot — the lacuna of a damaged inscription. Distinctive; risks looking like a bug.
Ⅷ · Bracketed
Crossbar ends turn up into short verticals — a lintel on two posts. Architectural, very stable, wide.

1c Marks Three directions. The first is used in 1a and 1b.
Ⅰ. Inscribed tau
A carved Τ: the serifs are notched away rather than added, the way a chisel leaves stone. Reads as a letter at 16px and as a mark at 512px.
Ⅱ. The reed
A stylus reduced to a stroke and its nib, resting on the ruled line. Warmest of the three; least legible at small sizes.
Ⅲ. Ruled speech
A waveform cut to the proportions of column rules — sound written down. Animates naturally while a job runs.
